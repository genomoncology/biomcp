# Biologic approvals match the exact product

Date: 2026-10-19. Clinical reviewer ruling for ticket 2270.

A biologic's FDA name with a shared core and a four-letter suffix shows the products are related, not that they are the same licensed product. BioMCP never attributes an approval to a drug on a suffix or shared core alone.

- DARZALEX (BLA761036) matches plain daratumumab by its exact daratumumab active ingredient.
- DARZALEX FASPRO (BLA761145) does not match plain daratumumab, because its active ingredients also include hyaluronidase. This keeps the brand and ingredient rule of 2043.
- BLENREP (BLA761440) matches belantamab mafodotin only through a source-confirmed BLENREP identity whose ingredient metadata agrees.

Show full source names and application and product numbers. Infer no indication or interchangeability. A related-products view is not part of this ruling.

Sources: FDA, Nonproprietary Naming of Biological Products, guidance for industry, January 2017 (https://www.fda.gov/files/drugs/published/Nonproprietary-Naming-of-Biological-Products-Guidance-for-Industry.pdf); Drugs@FDA application overviews for BLA761036, BLA761145 and BLA761440.

## Combination products

A follow-up clinical review kept approvals for an explicitly requested combination. They match only when the complete set of requested ingredient names, including suffixes and salts, equals the candidate product's complete ingredient set, and a source-owned brand or product identity belongs to that same product. Being named as a combination makes a product eligible; it is not proof. A product with additional, missing or unnamed ingredients is rejected, and so is an application product that does not match. So "daratumumab and hyaluronidase-fihj" matches DARZALEX FASPRO (BLA761145), and plain daratumumab still does not.

Sources: FDA Purple Book entry for BLA761145; DARZALEX FASPRO prescribing information, revision 761145s028, January 2026.
